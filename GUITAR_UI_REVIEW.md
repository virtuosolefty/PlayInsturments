# Guitar UI upgrade

Desktop review and implementation · 26 September 2026

The original view had a broad, nearly overhead neck, little separation between the strings and wood, an uneven five-chord selector, and a diagram that only explained finger numbers. The revised workspace keeps the existing practice engine and guitar voice path, while making the instrument and chord controls easier to read and play.

| Priority | Comparison and observed weakness | Implemented change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Explain the chord | [ChordBank's dot controls](https://chordbank.com/guides/dots/) switch between fingers, notes and intervals. Our diagram only showed fingers, leaving the musical meaning unclear. | A persistent Fingers / Notes / Intervals selector updates the diagram, 3D neck and accessible fret buttons together. Full chord names, chord-tone badges and copper root markers explain each voicing. Muted and open strings remain distinct. |
| 1 · Make auditioning obvious | [ChordBank](https://chordbank.com/guides/strumming-chords/) supports strumming and individual-string playback. [Musicca](https://www.musicca.com/guitar) also makes clicking strings and revealing note names central interactions. Our card only exposed whole-chord strums. | Six numbered string-preview buttons show the actual chord pitches. Muted strings are disabled; played strings turn teal. Free play also has six open-string reference buttons. Up/down strumming, strength and spread retain their existing behavior. |
| 1 · Improve the instrument silhouette | [Guitar Pro's instrument view](https://www.guitar-pro.com/docs/gp8/display-options/instrument-views) emphasizes clear string/fret positions and hover-note information. Our neck looked broad and flat, and its decorative controls competed with labels. | A slimmer Three.js neck, a lower viewing angle, darker rosewood, raised metal frets, cream binding and real cast shadows provide depth. Strings keep distinct gauges. Existing hover pitch, exact-position feedback and mirrored left-handed picking are retained. This 3D treatment is our design adaptation, not a claim that Guitar Pro uses the same rendering. |
| 2 · Organize the playing controls | The previous selector wrapped D onto a separate row, while chord name, note names, diagram and dynamics had weak grouping. | Five equal chord buttons; a dedicated diagram surface; separate string previews and strum actions; compact strength/spread controls. Warm neutral light mode and a charcoal dark mode frame the wood without recoloring every control. |
| 2 · Read tablature quickly | [Songsterr's help](https://www.songsterr.com/help) separates tab-reading guidance from transport shortcuts. Our thick duration bars and large circles dominated the tab. | Smaller outlined fret-number tiles, thinner duration stems, numbered string lanes, quieter grid lines and a consistent playhead. Bar numbers use the score's time signature. The exercise selector, playback controls and scoring stay in place. |
| 2 · Keep keyboard access and desktop fit | A polished visual instrument still needs a usable non-pointer path. | Roving arrow-key fret controls, visible focus, text-size choices, left-handed orientation, reduced-motion support and the 2D fallback remain available. Neck height is in the toolbar. The chord explorer becomes a horizontal workbench in Focus mode. Desktop layouts were checked at 1280×720 and 1440×900; the existing fit checks also cover 1366×768 and 1920×1080. |

Scope: desktop guitar presentation and interaction affordances. The five authored chord voicings, study notes, tuning, audio engine, strum scheduling, scoring and piano rendering are retained. No microphone tuner, alternate tuning system, new chord catalog or mobile redesign is implied.

Verification: 541 unit tests passed. Browser checks covered real audio-output activity, practice scoring, opposite strum orders and strength, exact and possible MIDI positions, mirrored 3D picking, keyboard navigation, chord label persistence, muted strings, WebGL fallback, light/dark themes, desktop sizing and Focus mode. A fresh development server is used by playwright.guitar.config.js to avoid stale HMR module URLs in MIDI subscriptions.
